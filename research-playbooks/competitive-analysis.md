# Research Playbook: Competitive Analysis

When the project will compete with — or borrow from — commercial products, this is how to study them quickly without wasting weeks.

## What you're trying to learn

Not "everything about competitor X." Specifically:

1. **What do they do well?** Things you should match or exceed.
2. **What do they do badly?** Gaps you can fill.
3. **What can you steal?** Patterns, language, UX choices that work.
4. **Why do their users pick them?** Real reasons, not marketing claims.
5. **Why do their users leave?** This is where opportunity lives.

## Information sources, ranked by signal

### High signal

- **The product itself.** Sign up, use it, take screenshots. Document where it shines and where it frustrates.
- **Cancellation reviews.** People who left tell you the real failure modes.
- **G2 / Capterra / TrustRadius reviews** filtered to 1-3 stars
- **Reddit threads** asking "X vs Y" or "alternatives to X"
- **Customer support forums** — patterns of recurring problems
- **Sales calls and demos** if accessible

### Medium signal

- **Pricing pages** — tells you their tiering logic and target customer
- **Documentation** — depth indicates product maturity and complexity
- **Changelogs / release notes** — shows what they prioritize
- **Job postings** — reveals what they're building next
- **LinkedIn employee count and trajectory**

### Low signal (but read for context)

- **Marketing site** — aspirational, not actual
- **Press coverage** — usually echoes their pitch
- **Top-rated reviews** — often gamed

## Capture template per competitor

```
## {{Competitor name}}

- **Link:** {{url}}
- **Pricing:** {{tiers and what they unlock}}
- **Target customer:** {{who they actually sell to, not the marketing claim}}
- **Core capabilities:** {{the 3-5 things they actually do}}
- **What they do well:** {{specific strengths}}
- **What they do badly:** {{specific weaknesses, with citations from reviews}}
- **Patterns worth stealing:** {{specific UX, language, or feature ideas}}
- **Why we're not them:** {{our differentiation in one sentence}}
```

## Time budget

For an early kickoff, spend 30-60 minutes per major competitor. Going deeper rarely changes the build plan more than the first hour did. Revisit after Phase 1 of the actual build, when you have more context for what matters.

## Anti-patterns

- **Feature-by-feature comparison spreadsheets.** Almost always a waste — the differentiator is rarely a feature, it's the experience or the audience.
- **Studying only the leader.** The #2-#5 players often signal where the market is moving.
- **Asking "what features should we have?"** — wrong frame. Ask "what job are users hiring this for, and which competitor does that job worst?"
- **Believing marketing copy.** Always corroborate with users.

## When to skip competitive analysis

- Solo internal tools where competition is irrelevant
- Highly niche projects where there are no real competitors (still worth a 15-min search to confirm)
- Projects where the user already deeply knows the competitive landscape from work experience
